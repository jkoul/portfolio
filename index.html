U.S. Census Bureau

data.census.gov

For six years, I was a senior frontend engineer and data visualization specialist for data.census.gov, the Census Bureau’s flagship open data platform. I guided overhauls of its most heavily trafficked, data-intensive views (the table, map, and profile pages), built visualizations like the population pyramid on those profiles, and mentored other developers along the way.

U.S. Census Bureau

Chart library & Chart page

Early in my tenure, I inherited inconsistent Chart.js implementations that broke on mobile and strayed from Census Data Visualization Standards. Over four years, largely without a mandate, I envisioned and built a custom chart library adapting D3.js to Vue’s reactivity, standards-aligned out of the box. It became the foundation for a new Chart page I led, which launched in December 2024 to praise from high-ranking Census officials.

Digital Democracy Project

DDP design system & React component library

DDP’s public website had grown into a mess of inconsistent design patterns. As I rebuild it as a Next.js application, I created a custom design system and React component library to enforce consistent, accessible patterns across the whole site. It ships as a lightweight npm package with Storybook documentation, so every future DDP product can start from the same foundation.

Urban Institute

From Camps to Campaign Funds

Amidst the IRS controversy around 501(c)(4) organizations in 2013, I penned a blog post for the Urban Institute aiming to dispel myths about the 501(c)(4) subsector, which directly inspired an article by Time’s Michael Grunwald. I later fleshed that commentary out into an in-depth, first-of-its-kind research report on the history, anatomy, and activities of the subsector.

About

One simple question has driven my career: how can we give ordinary people a stronger voice in public decisionmaking?

Early in my career, that question took me on a journey through grassroots advocacy, policy research, strategic communications, data science, and nonprofit management. About a decade ago, it led me into software engineering, data visualization, and civic tech product development, where I could build cutting-edge technology myself instead of simply advocating for it.
